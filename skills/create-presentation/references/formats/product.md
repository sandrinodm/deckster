# Format: product presentation, launch, roadmap, demo-led talk

Covers feature and product launches, roadmap presentations, product reviews and demo-led overviews, for internal or external audiences. For a pitch to a specific prospect, use `sales.md`. The evidence base here is mostly practice; the general findings on narrative, cognitive load and demos apply.

## The central discipline: problem before product

Teams that built something want to show it. Audiences want to know why they should care. Spend real time on the problem, for a specific person, before the product appears. A useful test: could the audience state the problem in their own words, and say what it costs, before the first screenshot? If not, the product will look like a solution to nothing.

Frame the problem as:

- **Who** has it (a named kind of user, ideally a real one).
- **What they are trying to do** and what gets in the way today.
- **What it costs** them: time, money, risk, frustration. Use real data or a real case.
- **Why now:** what changed that makes this solvable or urgent.

## Spine

1. **Hook:** the user's situation or the surprising fact.
2. **The problem and its cost.**
3. **The insight:** the one idea behind your approach. This is the big idea; it should be repeatable.
4. **The product, shown:** a demo or walkthrough organized around the user's goal, not the feature list. Get to it quickly. Screens go one per slide on the `screen` layout, large, with a few callouts at most.
5. **Proof it works:** results, early customers, benchmarks, before-and-after.
6. **What is and is not included:** scope, availability, pricing or rollout as relevant. Honest limits build trust.
7. **What happens next and what you need from this audience.**

Variants:

- **Launch (external):** contrast structure works well. Alternate the frustrating present with what is now possible, and end on the future state.
- **Roadmap:** organize by customer problems or themes, not by team or quarter. State confidence levels (committed, planned, exploring) and what was deliberately left out. A `columns` board of now, next and later suits it. For executives, answer first.
- **Product review (internal):** what we learned, what we shipped, what moved, what we will do next; data-forward, see `data-visuals.md`.

## Demo flow

Read the demo section of `slide-craft.md`. For this format:

- Script one scenario with a named user and a goal that follows from the problem slide.
- **Setup slide:** who we are watching, what they want, what to look for.
- **Demo:** three or so beats. Lead with the moment of value, not login and settings. Narrate the why, not the clicks.
- **Landing slide:** an assertion stating what the audience just saw ("From alert to root cause in under a minute, with no query written").
- Fallback recording in place, switch cue in the notes, a time budget and a bail-out point.
- If the product is not demoable, use annotated screenshots built in steps, or a before-and-after pair. One screen per slide with a highlight on what matters.

## Features and benefits

For each capability shown, complete the sentence: "which means that [user] can [outcome]". Put the outcome in the title and the capability in the evidence. Show three capabilities well rather than twelve in passing; the rest go in a single "also included" slide or the appendix.

## Audience adjustments

- **Customers and users:** their workflow, what changes for them, how to get it.
- **Executives:** the business outcome, the bet, the ask. Demo short or skipped.
- **Engineers:** how it works, the constraints, the trade-offs; see `technical-deep-dive.md`.
- **Sales and support:** who it is for, how to position it, what will be asked; see `sales.md`.
- **Press and analysts:** what is new, why it matters to the market, proof, availability.

## Pitfalls

- Starting with the product name and a feature list.
- A demo that tours menus.
- Benefits asserted without evidence.
- Roadmaps as dense Gantt charts.
- Hiding what is missing; someone will ask.
- Invented customer quotes or metrics. Use `[NEEDS: ...]`.
