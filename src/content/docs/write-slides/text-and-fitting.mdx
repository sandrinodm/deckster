Write slide text that fits the first time, and fix a slide whose text is too long, too small or cut off. You never set a font size: the layout picks it from the brand's type scale and steps it down when the content is long, and `deck lint` tells you when it still does not fit.

## How slide text is sized

Every slide is drawn on a 1920x1080 canvas and scaled to the screen, so sizes are canvas pixels. The brand's type scale names the sizes; the `default` brand's (the output goes on with spacing and surfaces, cut here):

```console
$ deck brand default
Default (brands/default/brand.ts)
Fonts: display Bricolage Grotesque, body Inter, mono JetBrains Mono
Type: display 160px, headline 80px, title 48px, lead 40px, body 36px, small 26px, caption 24px, label 22px, stat 184px, quote 84px, code 28px
...
```

Each layout sets each part in one of these steps: a content slide's title in `headline`, its eyebrow in `label`, running text in `body`, a statement in `display`. No text may be smaller than 20px on the canvas.

The kit adjusts these steps in three ways:

- **Titles and other single texts step down instead of wrapping further.** A long title shrinks in up to four visible steps (to 88%, 78% and 70% of its size) rather than taking a third line.
- **Sparse text steps up.** Running text with few words is set one or two steps larger (`lead` or `title` instead of `body`) when it still leaves room at the bottom.
- **Some layouts fit their whole content to the region.** They render at their largest level and, while the content overflows, step down one level before the slide is shown.

## Layouts that fit their content

| Layout | What steps down when the content is long |
| --- | --- |
| `statement` | Up to three lines are set in the display style; a longer statement is set at quote size, in four lines at most. With `size: giant`, the statement is set as large as fits, larger than the display style. |
| `cards` | The card text goes from the body size to the small size, then to compact cards (smaller tile and title, less padding). |
| `columns` | The items go from body to small size, then compact columns, then the caption size for a full board under a long title. |
| `steps` | Three steps start at the lead size, four at body, five at small; the last level is compact. |
| `timeline` | The items step down through the timeline's levels, largest first. |
| `bento` | Three tiles start at the body size, more at small; when the text does not fit the whole grid steps down, to compact tiles last. |
| `table` | A wide or long table steps down in size, never below the brand's smallest type. |
| `team` | Names get a step smaller and every line the small size, then smaller still. |
| `proof` | The metrics close up, then set their labels and figures a step smaller. |
| `shift` | The old state's text and both figures get a step smaller, then the new heading too. |
| `matrix` | The note beside the map steps down from the lead size. |
| `equation` | The whole row of figures and signs scales down together. |
| `screen` | The sentence and callout labels beside the screen step down. |
| `ask` | The amount steps down to the panel's width, and the plan until it fits. |

## When text is too long anyway

**Before rendering**, the build checks each layout's title limit and `deck lint` checks the slot budgets, in words, bullets and lines of code. A title over the limit stops the build:

```console
$ deck lint launch-review
decks/launch-review/slides/risks.mdx
  error   schema (risks): title: The cards layout sets at most 60 characters, got 74.
          Fix: Shorten the title to the point itself and move detail into the body or the notes.

launch-review: 1 error, 0 warnings.
```

and a slot over its budget is a `content-budget` error:

```console
$ deck lint launch-review
decks/launch-review/slides/claim.mdx
  error   content-budget (claim): The body slot holds 31 words; its layout allows 24.
          Fix: Cut it to the essentials, split the slide, move detail into speaker notes, or pick a layout made for more content (`deck layouts`).

launch-review: 1 error, 0 warnings in 2 slides (2 steps checked).
```

**After rendering**, `deck lint` measures every step of every slide:

| Rule | What it finds |
| --- | --- |
| `canvas-overflow` | Content past the edge of the canvas, clipped. |
| `container-overflow` | Content larger than its own container, clipped or spilling out. |
| `text-truncated` | Text cut short with an ellipsis or a line clamp. |
| `text-overlap` | Two pieces of text drawn over each other. |
| `min-font-size` | Text below the 20px minimum on the canvas. |

All five are errors. How to read a finding and fix it is on [lint](/review/lint/); why budgets count words is on [layouts and slots](/concepts/layouts-and-slots/).

## Keep text short

Write the title as the point, within the layout's limit (60 characters on `cards`, 48 on `code`; each layout's limit and budgets are on its page under [layouts](/reference/layouts/)). Make one point per slide and split a slide that makes two. Move detail into the notes, where the speaker can say it. When the content is a list or a comparison, pick a layout made for it from `deck layouts`.

## Automatic typography

- Straight quotes in prose and frontmatter text become typographic ones: `"` and `'` open after a space, a bracket or a dash and close elsewhere, and an apostrophe before a short year (`'26`) stays an apostrophe. Code, commands, URLs and QR values keep their straight quotes, and hyphens and dashes are left as written, so a flag such as `--env` survives.
- On the `statement` layout, words you mark with `*...*` are set in italics; `emphasis: underline` sets them in the accent over a heavy rule instead, which reads on every surface. Mark the two or three words that carry the claim.
