Use the `proof` layout for a customer story: a named person saying what changed, beside the one to three numbers that show it. The title states the result, the quote takes the wider left side with the person and their company's logo under it, and the metrics stand on an accent panel that runs to the right edge.

## Example

<SlideExample deck="lookbook" slide="proof-st-brenna" />

Notice how little the slide needs: one metric with `from:`, shown as 74 min → 31 min, a `timeframe:` above it, and no `photo:`, so an accent rule stands where the portrait would be. The title already says the result; the quote says why it matters.

## When to use it

- A named customer has numbers. `name:` and `metrics:` are both required: one to three metrics, the strongest first, since the first is set largest.
- The quote is short. The body is two or three sentences in the customer's voice, at most 36 words; the layout adds the quotation marks.
- The result fits the title: up to 52 characters, stated as the outcome ("Samples reach the lab in 31 minutes, not 74").
- You want to walk through the numbers. `reveal: true` shows the quote first, then brings in the panel with the lead metric and each other metric on the next step.

Pick another layout when one half is missing:

- Words without numbers: use [`quote`](/reference/layouts/quote/).
- Numbers without a voice: use [`stats`](/reference/layouts/stats/).
- A list of customers: use [`logos`](/reference/layouts/logos/).

## Common mistakes

- **A long quote.** A 51-word quote fails [`deckster lint`](/review/lint/) with `content-budget`: "The body slot holds 51 words; its layout allows 36." Keep the sentence that names the change and move the rest to the notes.
- **A long title.** The title has a 52-character limit, checked before the slide renders: "title: The proof layout sets at most 52 characters, got 54." State the result, not the story.
- **Numbers in the quote.** The figures belong in `metrics:`, each with `value`, `label` and optionally `from`, so the panel can set them large and show the before and after. Write the quote without them.
- **Long figures.** A metric's `value` and `from` take at most 8 characters as text (`38%`, `$1.2M`, `11 days`), and its `label` at most 40. Put the unit in the value and what it measures in the label.
- **Quotation marks in the body.** The layout sets hanging quotation marks of its own, so write the words without them.
- **A source left out.** Put where the numbers come from in `<Footnote>`, up to 16 words, as every lookbook proof slide does.
