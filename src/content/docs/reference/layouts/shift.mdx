Use the `shift` layout for the one change a talk argues for: a new process, a new cost, a new owner. The old state (`from:`) stands on the left, the new state (`to:`) on an accent panel that runs to the right and bottom edges, with an arrow on the seam between them.

## Example

<SlideExample deck="lookbook" slide="shift-cost" />

Notice that both states fill the same four parts, a `label`, a `value`, a `title` and a `text`, and that the rows line up across the seam: €6.40 sits level with €3.10, so the eye compares like with like. The old state is set smaller and muted; the new one is larger, on the panel.

## When to use it

- There is one change and two states. `from:` and `to:` are both required, and each needs a `title` (up to 36 characters). `label` (up to 24), `value` (a figure as shown, up to 8 characters) and `text` (up to 80) are optional.
- Both sides need a sentence of why. When the change is a single number, the `<Comparison>` chart says it with less.
- You want the room to see the old way first. `reveal: true` shows the old state, then brings in the panel, the arrow and the new state on the next step.
- The title (up to 60 characters) can say the change outright: "A drop costs half of what a van costs".

When two or three options are measured on several rows, it is not one change: use the [`comparison`](/reference/layouts/comparison/) layout.

## Common mistakes

- **A long body.** The body is one sentence under the title on why the change matters, at most 14 words. A 19-word sentence fails [`deck lint`](/review/lint/) with `content-budget`: "The body slot holds 19 words; its layout allows 14." Move the reasons into each state's `text`, or into the notes.
- **A missing state.** A slide with only `to:` is a `schema` error before it renders: "from is required." Its fix lists what `from:` takes.
- **The states in the body.** Write the two states in `from:` and `to:`, not as bullets in the body; the body takes text only.
- **Long state text.** Each state's `text` takes at most 80 characters. When the text still does not fit, the layout sets the old state's text and both figures a step smaller, then the new heading too; shorter text keeps the larger sizes.

## Related layouts

- [`comparison`](/reference/layouts/comparison/): two or three options measured on the same two to five rows.
- [`stats`](/reference/layouts/stats/): two to four figures side by side, with no before and after.
- [`timeline`](/reference/layouts/timeline/): a change in dated milestones rather than two states.

For a change that is one number, see [`<Comparison>`](/reference/charts/#props-Comparison). How `reveal` stages a slide is on [builds](/write-slides/builds/).
