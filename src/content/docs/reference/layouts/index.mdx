Pick a layout for what the slide needs to say or show. Set its name in frontmatter, for example `layout: stats`. A slide with no `layout` uses `default`. Each of the 63 kit layouts has its own page with a rendered example, the source, and the fields, slots and budgets. See [Layouts and slots](/concepts/layouts-and-slots/) for how the contract works and [MDX slides](/write-slides/mdx-slides/) for how to fill it.

## Choose a layout

If your brand brings a template, run `deck layouts <deck> --summary` to see its layouts first. Use a brand layout if its fixed regions fit the slide's message and content. The kit layouts cover the other slides. [Brand layouts](/brands/brand-layouts/)

| What the audience needs | Which layout to try |
| --- | --- |
| Where they are in the talk | Use [`title`](/reference/layouts/title/) for the cover, [`section`](/reference/layouts/section/) for a part divider, or [`agenda`](/reference/layouts/agenda/) to preview the order. |
| One idea to take in | Use [`statement`](/reference/layouts/statement/) for a single sentence on its own, [`content`](/reference/layouts/content/) for a headline with a short explanation, or [`split`](/reference/layouts/split/) when a visual needs to sit beside that explanation. |
| A set of related ideas | Use [`cards`](/reference/layouts/cards/) for two to four ideas with equal weight, [`bento`](/reference/layouts/bento/) when one facet needs the most space, or [`columns`](/reference/layouts/columns/) for short lists under separate headings. |
| Evidence for a takeaway | Use [`stats`](/reference/layouts/stats/) when two to four headline numbers carry the point, [`chart`](/reference/layouts/chart/) when a single figure shows the pattern, or [`dashboard`](/reference/layouts/dashboard/) when the point depends on several figures. [Choosing a chart](/data-and-diagrams/choosing-a-chart/) |
| A comparison or change | Use [`comparison`](/reference/layouts/comparison/) to assess two or three options against the same criteria, [`shift`](/reference/layouts/shift/) to argue for a move from one state to another, or [`before-after`](/reference/layouts/before-after/) to show the change in two pictures. |
| How something works | Use [`steps`](/reference/layouts/steps/) for a short ordered procedure, [`diagram`](/reference/layouts/diagram/) for a flow, sequence or structure, or [`service-blueprint`](/reference/layouts/service-blueprint/) to show who owns a handoff across a customer journey. |
| A product screen or demo | Use [`screen`](/reference/layouts/screen/) for one whole screenshot, [`screen-story`](/reference/layouts/screen-story/) to focus on a detail, show a handoff or walk through a short workflow, or [`demo`](/reference/layouts/demo/) for an interactive component. [Presentation patterns](/examples/presentation-patterns/) |
| How the plan unfolds | Use [`timeline`](/reference/layouts/timeline/) for dated milestones, [`schedule`](/reference/layouts/schedule/) for durations across lanes, [`roadmap-transit`](/reference/layouts/roadmap-transit/) for dependencies between workstreams, or [`roadmap-hill`](/reference/layouts/roadmap-hill/) for how certain the approach is. |
| Something to recall or practise | Use [`recap`](/reference/layouts/recap/) to leave two to four takeaways, [`quiz`](/reference/layouts/quiz/) to check an answer, or [`exercise`](/reference/layouts/exercise/) to give the audience a timed task. |
| What to decide or do next | Use [`ask`](/reference/layouts/ask/) for one resource request with milestones, [`actions`](/reference/layouts/actions/) to name owners and due dates, or [`closing`](/reference/layouts/closing/) for the final takeaway and how to follow up. |

Open the layout's page before writing the slide. Check the title limit, required fields, slot types and budgets. If the slide needs to carry two takeaways, give each its own slide. For a brand layout, start with its example from `deck layouts <deck> --json` and check which slots its regions bind in `layouts.ts`. [MDX body content in brand layouts](/brands/brand-layouts/#mdx-body-content)

## How to read a layout page

Each layout page explains when to use the layout and shows a lookbook slide with the source file that made it. The reference entry lists the fields, slots and budgets to keep at hand while you author a slide.

- **Frontmatter, surface, title.** The line to write, the surface the slide gets unless its frontmatter sets `surface`, and whether `title:` is required and how many characters it may have. "Optional" with no limit means the title names the slide in the outline and may not be shown.
- **PowerPoint.** The PowerPoint slide layout `deck export --pptx` maps the slide to, so its title and body stay editable placeholders.
- **Description.** What the layout is for and how it sets its content.
- **Slots.** Each named region: the tag you write it with (`body` is the content outside any slot tag), what it accepts (text, bullets, an image, a component), and its budget in words, bullets or code lines. A `*` or a `required` mark means `deck lint` reports a `schema` error when the slot is empty. The budgets are the same in every brand; `deck lint` holds each slide to them.
- **Fields.** The layout's own frontmatter keys, with their default and allowed values. The keys every slide takes are on [slide frontmatter](/reference/slide-frontmatter/).
- **Variants.** A variant applies when the slide matches its condition (a field set, a slot filled, a number of items), and tightens the budget it lists.
- **Example.** A complete slide in that layout: `deck new slide <deck> <id> --layout <name>` writes this example as the new slide. It is the catalog's example, not the published slide under the page's own Example heading.

The same data comes from the CLI for the layouts your workspace has. `deck layouts <deck> --summary` prints one compact entry per layout:

```console
$ deck layouts launch-review --summary
default       surface default   title any
              One column of content under an accent rule, centred on the slide: a heading with a lead paragraph, or a heading with a short list.
              slots: body* heading/text/bullets/image/component 80 words 6 bullets
title         surface gradient  title <= 36
              The cover of a deck: the deck title set large on the bottom line, a one-line subtitle, and who is presenting and when.
              slots: body text 14 words; <Media> image
...
```

The output above is cut after two layouts. `deck layouts <deck> --json` prints everything the reference entries show, for every layout.
