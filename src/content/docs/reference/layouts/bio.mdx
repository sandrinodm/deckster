Use the `bio` layout to introduce one person, usually yourself, near the start of a talk. You get a portrait down the left side, the person's name and role beside it, a few facts that say why the room should listen, and up to two ways to reach them.

## When to use it

- One person, once, near the start. The catalog's guidance is to use it once; it is the slide that says who is speaking before the argument begins.
- The facts fit in 28 words or three bullets. That is the body's budget; anything longer belongs in the speaker notes.
- `name:` is the only required field. `role:` (up to 48 characters), `photo:` and `contacts:` (at most two) are optional, so a slide with only a name and a sentence is valid.

Pick another layout when the slide is about more than one person or about what they said:

- Three or more people: use [`team`](/reference/layouts/team/), which the `bio` description names for that case.
- Someone speaking for your point: use [`quote`](/reference/layouts/quote/), which sets their words, not their CV.
- A customer with numbers: use [`proof`](/reference/layouts/proof/).
- A user profile from research, with goals and frustrations: use [`persona`](/reference/layouts/persona/).

## Common mistakes

- **Too many facts.** A fourth bullet fails [`deck lint`](/review/lint/) with `content-budget`: "The body slot holds 4 bullets; its layout allows 3." Keep the best three, or turn them into one sentence of at most 28 words.
- **A third contact.** `contacts:` takes at most two entries. A third is a `schema` error before the slide renders: "contacts: Expected at most 2 contacts, got a list of 3 items." Each contact needs `label` (up to 16 characters) and `text` (up to 40); `href` is optional and makes it a link.
- **Expecting `title:` on the slide.** The name is the heading. Write the person's name in `name:` (up to 32 characters) and use `title:` for how the slide reads in the outline, such as "Who is speaking".
- **A face cropped off.** The portrait is cropped to a tall frame, keeping the centre of the upper third unless you say otherwise. When the face sits elsewhere, set `focal:` as a CSS position, such as `focal: 40% 20%`.
- **A portrait in brand colours.** The portrait takes the brand's default image treatment. For a picture that must keep its own colours, set `treatment: plain`, which every brand has.

## Related layouts

- [`team`](/reference/layouts/team/): three or more people on one slide instead of one.
- [`quote`](/reference/layouts/quote/): what someone said, not who they are.
- [`proof`](/reference/layouts/proof/): a named customer with a quote and the numbers behind it.
- [`title`](/reference/layouts/title/): the cover that comes before the bio, with the presenter in `byline:`.
- [`persona`](/reference/layouts/persona/): a user persona from research, with a portrait, a quotation, goals and frustrations.

How paths in `photo:` resolve, and what `deck lint` checks about images, is on [media](/write-slides/media/).
