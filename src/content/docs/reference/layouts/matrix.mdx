Use the `matrix` layout for a two-by-two map: two axes that cross in the middle, three to ten items placed by judgement, and the one the slide is about marked. Make a positioning or competition slide with it, or any choice framed by two trade-offs.

Notice that only Saltby, marked `us: true`, is set in the accent with a ring, and its quadrant is tinted; the other five stay muted. This map has no `quadrants:` captions, because the axis labels say enough. The plot takes the right five columns, and the note and the source stand to its left.

## When to use it

- **The positions are judgements, not measurements.** `x` and `y` run from 0 at the low end to 100 at the high end, and the plot shows no values. Say in `<Footnote>` who placed the items and how.
- **Three to ten items.** Each has a `name` of up to 18 characters, or 14 once there are seven or more items.
- **Two axes you can name in a few words.** `x:` and `y:` each take a `low` and a `high` label of up to 16 characters. `quadrants:` is optional: exactly four captions of up to 24 characters.
- **One subject.** Mark exactly one item `us: true`. With `reveal: true` the axes come first, then the other items, then the marked one with the note.

The note beside the plot says what the map means, in up to 40 words. When the positions are measured, plot them with `<Scatter>` in the `chart` layout. When options are scored on several rows, use the `comparison` layout.

## Common mistakes

- **Two items marked `us`.** Only one item can be the subject. A second is a [`schema` error](/reference/lint-rules/): "items[2].us: More than one item is marked `us`: "Saltby", "Fallowmere"."
- **Dots on top of each other.** Items must stand at least 6 units apart. Closer than that is an error that names both: "items[2]: "Fallowmere" (70, 61) stands on top of "Ardwen" (68, 58)." Move one, or merge them if they stand for the same thing.
- **Captions in the wrong corners.** `quadrants:` is read top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right. A list in another order puts "Where we want to be" in the wrong quadrant, and lint cannot tell.
- **Crowded corners.** Names find room beside their dots by themselves. When the quadrants have captions, keep items out of the corners where the captions sit.
- **Long names on a full plot.** At seven items or more, every name is held to 14 characters.

## Related layouts

- [`chart`](/reference/layouts/chart/): a `<Scatter>` when the positions are measured values.
- [`comparison`](/reference/layouts/comparison/): two or three options scored on the same rows.

For when a measured plot is the better form, see [choosing a chart](/data-and-diagrams/choosing-a-chart/).
