A slide never sets its own grid, type sizes or spacing. It names a layout, and the layout places the content. After reading this page you will know what a layout promises, which content goes in which slot, how much text a slide can hold, and how to choose a layout for a message, either yourself or through an agent.

## A slide picks a layout and fills it

The `layout:` key in a slide's frontmatter picks the layout. The rest of the frontmatter and the content below it fill that layout. The layout owns the grid, the type and the spacing, and takes all of them from the brand. A `cards` slide, for example, gives each card an icon, a title and a line of text in its frontmatter, and one sentence in its body:

<SlideExample deck="layouts" slide="cards-principles" />

The slide says nothing about where the cards go or how large the text is. The build checks `cards` against the layout, looks up each `icon` in the brand's icon set, and fails on a name the set doesn't have.

The kit has 63 layouts, from `title`, `section` and `statement` to `stats`, `split`, `timeline` and `closing`. The engine ships a plain `default` layout, and the kit replaces it with its own. The [layouts reference](/reference/layouts/) shows every one. Twenty of them, such as `quiz`, `pricing` and `schedule`, are new in 0.6.0, and each of their pages says so.

## The contract

Each layout publishes a contract, which the build and `deck lint` check slides against:

- **Frontmatter fields:** the keys the layout takes beyond the ones every slide has, with their types, allowed values and defaults. `stats` takes a `stats` list of two to four items.
- **Title:** whether the slide needs a `title:` and its maximum length in characters, such as 90 for `content` and 44 for `split`.
- **Slots:** where content goes, which slots are required, and what each one accepts (text, bullets, an image, a chart and so on).
- **Default surface:** the [brand surface](/concepts/brands-and-surfaces/) a slide gets when it names none. A `section` divider is on `accent` and a `statement` is on `inverse`. A slide's own `surface:` wins.
- **Content budget:** the most words and bullets per slot, and the most lines for code.
- **Variants:** tighter limits that apply to some slides of a layout, such as a row of four stats, where each label has room for 32 characters instead of 48.

## Slots

A slot is a named place in a layout. In MDX, slide content outside the named slot tags fills `body`. A [brand layout](/brands/brand-layouts/#mdx-body-content) only has a `body` slot if it binds a region to it. Other slots are filled with a tag named after the slot at the top level of the slide: `<Media>`, `<Aside>`, `<Footnote>`, `<Attribution>` and `<Caption>` in the kit's layouts. A TSX slide fills them by exporting a function with the same name.

This `content` slide puts its bullets in the body and its sources in `<Footnote>`, which the layout sets on the bottom line:

<SlideExample deck="exemplars" slide="why-eastgate" />

Each layout has its own set of slots: `split` has `<Media>`, `<Caption>` and `<Footnote>`, and `content` has `<Aside>` and `<Footnote>`. A missing required slot fails the build, and so does a slot tag the layout doesn't have:

```console
$ deck build launch-review
transforming...
✓ 852 modules transformed.
✗ Build failed in 549ms
The slide does not fit its layout's slots in decks/launch-review/slides/why-now.mdx:
  - <Caption> on line 8 is not a slot of the content layout, which has slots <Aside>, <Footnote>.
    Fix: Use a layout that has it (split, image), or move the content into one of this layout's slots.
```

The kinds of content a slot accepts are checked on the rendered slide by `deck lint`'s `slot-content` rule. [MDX slides](/write-slides/mdx-slides/) shows how to write frontmatter and slot tags.

## Budgets count content, not space

A budget counts words, bullets and lines of code. The `content` body, for example, holds at most 72 words and 6 bullets. It never counts how much room the content takes. Words and bullets are the same in every brand, so a slide that is within budget in one brand is within budget in all of them. [`deck lint`](/review/lint/) reports a slide over budget with the `content-budget` rule:

```console
$ deck lint launch-review
decks/launch-review/slides/why-now.mdx
  error   content-budget (why-now): The body slot holds 7 bullets; its layout allows 6.
          Fix: Cut it to the essentials, split the slide, move detail into speaker notes, or pick a layout made for more content (`deck layouts`).

launch-review: 1 error, 0 warnings in 2 slides (2 steps checked).
```

Whether the text fits a brand's type is a separate question, which the overflow rules answer: `container-overflow`, `canvas-overflow` and `text-truncated`. [Text and fitting](/write-slides/text-and-fitting/) covers what to do when a slide is within budget but still too full, and [lint as the contract](/concepts/lint-as-the-contract/) explains how the rules fit together.

## Choosing a layout

Start with what the audience needs from the slide. Use the chooser to find a layout for that job, then check its slots and budgets. [Choose a layout](/reference/layouts/#choose-a-layout)

`deck layouts --summary` lists the deck's [brand layouts](/brands/brand-layouts/) first, followed by the kit layouts. Each entry lists its default surface, title limit, slots with what they accept, and budget. An asterisk marks a required slot. A workspace using the default brand starts with these entries.

```console
$ deck layouts launch-review --summary
default       surface default   title any
              One column of content under an accent rule, centred on the slide: a heading with a lead paragraph, or a heading with a short list.
              slots: body* heading/text/bullets/image/component 80 words 6 bullets
title         surface gradient  title <= 36
              The cover of a deck: the deck title set large on the bottom line, a one-line subtitle, and who is presenting and when.
              slots: body text 14 words; <Media> image
section       surface accent    title <= 40
              A divider that opens a part of the talk: its number set large at the top, the part title on the bottom line and an optional one-sentence summary, whose two lines are kept free when it is left out so every divider sets its title at the same height.
              slots: body text 16 words; <Media> image
statement     surface inverse   title any
              One sentence set large, on its own: the claim the talk turns on, or a quote.
              slots: body* text/quote 24 words; <Attribution> text 12 words
...
```

An agent reads the full catalog with `deck layouts --json`. It holds every field with its default, allowed values and fix, every slot, budget and variant, and a complete example slide for each layout. That way an agent writes to the same limits the build and `deck lint` enforce.
