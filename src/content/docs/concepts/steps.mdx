A slide can show its content in stages: a list one point at a time, a chart one series at a time, a diagram one hop at a time. All of them run on one counter, the slide's step. After reading this page you will know where a slide's step count comes from, what moving forward and back does, and what screenshots and exports show. [Builds](/write-slides/builds/) shows how to write them.

## One counter per slide

Each slide has one integer step that starts at 0, where nothing that waits for a step is shown. Content opts in: `<Step at={1}>` reveals what it wraps from step 1 on. Charts, diagram nodes and edges, and annotations take an `at` prop that does the same. `at` is required and must be an integer of 1 or more. A bare `<Step>` fails the build of an MDX slide and renders an error in a TSX slide.

Text, charts, diagrams, code and video all read the same counter, so one press can reveal a bullet and the chart series it talks about together. Here four points come in one per step:

## Where the step count comes from

A slide has as many steps as its content asks for, plus step 0; nothing in the frontmatter sets the number. These register steps:

- `<Step at={n}>` and the `at` prop on charts, chart series, highlights, annotations and diagram parts;
- a code fence with highlight ranges, one step per range (`{3-5|4|7}` gives three);
- the move from one version to the next in a `<CodeMove>`;
- the commands of a `<Terminal>`, the cues of a `<Video>` and the steps an `<AppFrame>` says its app has;
- a layout's items when the slide sets `reveal: true`, which the `cards`, `timeline` and `steps` layouts and others allow.

Distinct `at` values become consecutive deck steps, so a gap never leaves a dead press. A slide with `<Step at={1}>` and `<Step at={3}>` has steps 0, 1 and 2, and deck step 2 reveals `at={3}`. Slide code still sees the numbers it wrote: `useStep()` returns 3 there.

## Moving forward and back

The right arrow, Space and Page Down move one step. From a slide's last step, the next press opens the next slide at step 0. Moving back walks the steps down, and from step 0 it opens the previous slide at its last step, with everything shown.

The URL hash holds both numbers: `#/<slide>/<step>`, with the slide counted from 1 and the step from 0. Every move rewrites it, so a reload or a shared link opens the same slide at the same step.

## What screenshots and exports show

`deck shot` and `deck export` render the deck in [static mode](/concepts/static-mode-and-readiness/) and capture each slide at its last step, with everything shown. So a slide that builds must read on its own when complete: that is what a PNG or a PDF page shows. To capture every stage:

- `deck shot <deck> --steps` writes one PNG per step, named like `02-steps.step0.png`.
- `deck export <deck> --steps` writes one PDF page or PPTX slide per step.
- A PPTX export turns steps into PowerPoint click animations. `--no-animations` turns that off; see [PowerPoint](/export-and-publish/powerpoint/).

`deck lint` measures every step of every slide, so content that overflows only at an intermediate step is still caught.

## Steps follow the talk

Add steps when the speaker explains something in parts: one step per part, in the order the notes explain them. Leave a slide whole when nothing on it is explained part by part. The notes of the chart above mark each press with a `[click]` cue: "This year starts the same. [click] In April the decks move to code. [click] By autumn a deck needs two rounds, a drop of more than half." In a deck with four or more chart, diagram and code slides, `deck lint` warns with `few-builds` when fewer than 25% of them build in steps. See [speaker notes](/write-slides/speaker-notes/), and the [deck API reference](/reference/deck-api/) for the hash and `window.__deck`.
